Recruiting pipeline stages: example and metrics

A clear pipeline tells you at any moment how many candidates you have, which stage they are in and where you are losing time. Here is how to build one.

Updated October 2026

What a recruiting pipeline is

A recruiting pipeline is the sequence of stages a candidate goes through, from first contact to hire (or rejection). Once it is defined, you see at a glance how many people are at each step and where work piles up.

Typical stages

StageWhat happensWhat to check
New / sourcedThe profile enters the databaseSource and privacy consent
ContactedFirst message or callReply within 3-5 days, otherwise follow up
ScreeningCall to verify motivation, salary, notice period, requirementsFactual notes
InterviewIn-depth meeting (video or on site)Written assessment right after
Submitted to clientProfile sent to the clientFeedback expected by a date
Client interviewOne or more meetings with the clientPrepare the candidate, collect feedback
OfferSalary and contract proposalNegotiation, response time
Hired / rejectedClosingReason, to learn and for data

There is no list that fits everyone: an agency working for several clients will have more stages than an in-house recruiter. What matters is that they are few, clear and unambiguous.

How to design your pipeline

  • One stage = one verifiable fact. "Interview done" is clear, "under evaluation" is not.
  • At most 7-9 stages. More, and you stop updating them.
  • Include exit states: rejected, declined by candidate, on hold. They matter for data and for future re-contact.
  • One pipeline per type of work if needed: hiring a technical profile and a salesperson may need different steps.

Metrics that matter

  • Conversion rate between one stage and the next: shows where you lose most candidates.
  • Average time per stage: candidates cool off if they wait too long.
  • Source of hired candidates: tells you where to invest your time.
  • Time to hire: from first contact to signature.

Most common mistakes

  • Too many stages, or stage names that change from day to day.
  • Candidates left for weeks in the same stage with no follow-up date.
  • Rejections with no reason recorded: you lose the chance to reuse the profile.
  • No overview: knowing "where we stand" means opening twenty records.

Frequently asked questions

How many stages should a recruiting pipeline have?

Usually 6 to 9, including exit states. More than that becomes hard to keep updated.

What is the difference between a pipeline and an ATS?

The pipeline is the candidate's path; an ATS (or recruiter CRM) is the software that manages it, with candidates, CVs, follow-ups, messages and statistics.

What is a Kanban view?

A board with one column per stage where you drag candidate cards. It shows immediately where work is concentrated.

Can I customise the stages in Recruiter CRM?

Yes, the pipeline stages are customisable so you can adapt them to your process.
