LinkedIn X-ray search for recruiters: find profiles with Google

A free way to search public LinkedIn profiles using Google. The formula, the operators that matter, and five searches you can copy and adapt.

Updated November 2026

What X-ray search is

An X-ray search is a Google search limited to one website. You ask Google to look only inside the public profile pages of LinkedIn, and to match the words that appear in them: role, skills, city. It is useful when the search inside LinkedIn is limited, or when you want to search by combinations of skills that the filters do not offer.

Keep in mind what it can and cannot do. Google only shows public pages it has already indexed, so you will see a part of the profiles, not all of them. It is a way to find names, not a complete database.

The basic formula

site:linkedin.com/in ("data engineer" OR "data developer") (Python AND SQL) Milan

Read it as: public LinkedIn profiles, with one of these two role titles, with both skills, mentioning Milan.

The operators you need

OperatorWhat it doesExample
site:Limits the search to one site or foldersite:linkedin.com/in

Five searches to copy

Replace the roles, skills and cities with your own. These are examples with made-up combinations.

1. A role in a city

The starting point for most searches.

site:linkedin.com/in "project manager" Turin

2. Several titles for the same role

The same job is written in many ways. Cover them in one search.

site:linkedin.com/in ("data engineer" OR "data developer" OR "big data engineer") Python SQL

3. Exclude students and interns

Removes junior profiles when you are looking for experience.

site:linkedin.com/in "java developer" Rome -student -intern -trainee -stage

4. Another country

Use the country subdomain and write the role in the local language too.

site:de.linkedin.com/in ("sales manager" OR "vertriebsleiter") Munich

5. Leave out a company

Useful to avoid your own company, a competitor or your client.

site:linkedin.com/in "account manager" Milan -"Company Name"

A simple workflow

  1. Read the position and pick the must-haves. Two or three skills at most. Every extra word removes results.
  2. List the role titles. Developer, engineer, programmer: write the variants people really use.
  3. Start wide, then narrow. Run the search with role and city, then add one skill at a time.
  4. Read the snippet before opening. The text under each result already shows role, city and often skills.
  5. Note who is worth contacting and write a short, personal message. See the guide to LinkedIn messages for recruiters.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Too many keywords. Five skills at once give zero results. Use the two most important.
  • Only one title. You miss everyone who writes the role differently.
  • Using automatic scraping tools. LinkedIn prohibits it and can restrict your account. Run the search and read the results yourself.
  • Sending the same message to everyone. It shows, and it hurts your reputation.
  • Keeping data with no reason. Save only the people you actually contact, and delete the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Is X-ray search legal?

Searching Google for public pages is normal. What matters is how you use the data: personal data is covered by privacy rules such as GDPR, so contact people with a clear reason, keep only what you need and delete it when it is no longer useful. Check the rules that apply to you.

Why do I see so few results?

Google only shows the public profiles it has indexed, and not all of them. Try other role titles, fewer keywords, or the same search on Bing.

Can I automate it with a tool?

Be careful: automated scraping of LinkedIn is against its terms and can get your account restricted. Running the search yourself and reading the results is safer.

Does it work outside Italy and the US?

Yes. Use the country subdomain, for example de.linkedin.com for Germany, and write the role in the local language as well as in English.
